Executive Summary

Public data on past PSA Code Sprint finalists is thin — no pitch decks, no judge quotes, and only one edition (2023) has real technical detail, from two participant-published GitHub READMEs rather than official sources. The one thing verified directly and with high confidence is the official judging rubric: four equally-billed categories — Innovation & Creativity, UI/UX, Technology, Presentation — with no disclosed weighting [5][6] verified.

That single fact is more actionable than anything found about specific past teams: presentation polish is not a tiebreaker, it's a quarter of the score.

The best-evidenced data point, 2023 champion opPORTunity, is instructive on craft: it isolated its riskiest component (an LLM chatbot) into its own simple Streamlit shell rather than deeply integrating it everywhere [1] verified — an instinct Portwatch's own build plan already shares. Confirmed champions across editions solved named, visible, real operational pain points with a clear before/after story, not abstract tech showcases.

Biggest caveat: no judge quotes, scored rubrics, or post-mortems were found for any edition — treat "what past winners built" as scoping/craft inspiration only, never as proof of what judges specifically valued. The verified rubric is the reliable signal.

Build & Demo Scope

Only 2023 yielded real technical detail. 2023 champion Team ByTheSea's opPORTunity was an HR-transformation portal with three named AI modules (PortBOT, LightHouse, PortConnections), built on Vue.js, FastAPI, MySQL, and a LangChain + ChromaDB + OpenAI AI layer, orchestrated via Docker Compose [1] high. A separate, simpler Streamlit app handled the chatbot-facing demo — the team isolated its highest-risk component rather than fully integrating it [1] medium — inferred from architecture. A second 2023 finalist, HR-Gen2, independently built a similar AI-HR concept on React/Firebase/GPT-4 [2] high.

The official results archive names champions/runners-up for 2022–2025 by team and institution, but publishes no project descriptions for any year beyond what participants separately posted on GitHub [3][4] high. No Devpost page, technical writeup, or retrospective blog was found — a genuine thin-data result, not a search failure.

Pitch & Presentation Craft

Official judging categories, verified by direct fetch: Innovation & Creativity (uniqueness), UI/UX (appeal, ease of interaction), Technology (functionality, technical difficulty), Presentation (clarity/flow/organisation, communication style) [5] high — directly verified. No numeric weighting is disclosed [6] high — directly verified.

opPORTunity's naming pattern is worth noting: each module got a memorable name tied to one pain point, mapped to a business theme (Talent/Experience/Engagement) [1] high. No pitch deck, time limit, or Q&A format was found; the event runs briefings → problem statements → build → a live "FINALS" presentation, confirmed at the format level only [1][9] low — metadata only.

Generic enterprise-hackathon judging patterns recur across marketing blogs [13][14][15] low — explicitly not PSA-specific — included only as a fallback, kept separate from the verified PSA rubric above.

Judge/Audience Reception & Trajectory

Confirmed champions: 2022 "Long Ken Kai My" (NUS) — team/institution confirmed officially [3] high, project described as PSAccess (a PON-digitization portal cutting paper waste) at medium confidence since that description rests only on a participant page and a PSA social post [7][8]; 2023 "ByTheSea" for opPORTunity [1] high; 2024 "TELETUBBIES" (NYP), project details unpublished [4]. 2025's winner could not be confirmed (Facebook login wall) [10][11] unverified; 2021 unconfirmed.

No judge quotes, scored criteria, or post-mortem commentary were found for any edition unverified — clean absence. An inferred (not judge-stated) pattern across the two well-evidenced champions: both solved a visible, real PSA-operational problem rather than a generic tech demo [1][7][8] — flagged as inference, not a sourced judging-criteria claim.

Trajectory: one 2023 team member's LinkedIn title suggests a possible post-hackathon hire, unconfirmed (profile blocked) [12] unverified. No evidence any winning project was piloted/adopted in production, or continued as a startup.

Cross-Dimension Insight

The three dimensions combine into one insight none alone would give: the verified rubric treats Presentation and UI/UX as equal to Technology, and both well-evidenced champions' actual winning trait, as best as public evidence shows, was a clearly-told, visibly-real operational story rather than technical ambition — while the one team with visible engineering discipline (opPORTunity's demo-isolation pattern) also happened to win. Read together: judges likely reward legible, reliable, real-world-framed execution over raw technical scope.
