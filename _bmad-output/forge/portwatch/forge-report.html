Portwatch

Forge session — pressure-tested for PSA Code Sprint hackathon viability

Outcome

Portwatch survived pressure-testing and came out sharper. The original 31-section proposal had real strengths (tiered autonomy, policy engine separate from the LLM, an execution trace) but three load-bearing claims were unexamined: whether it was actually agentic or a single agent with an if-statement, where the "confidence" numbers came from, and whether "scalable" and "original" were earned or just asserted. All three now have concrete answers baked into the design, plus a build schedule sized to a 6-day, 4-person team.

Locked decisions

Genuine multi-agent architecture

3 core specialist agents (Berth/Vessel, Crane, Yard) + optional cut-first 4th (AGV/Gate), each an independent LLM call with scoped least-privilege tools, run in parallel with per-agent timeouts. Portwatch itself is a separate arbiter agent that synthesizes conflicting specialist outputs into Options A/B/C. Deterministic policy engine unchanged, gates the chosen option outside LLM authority.

Confidence score is computed, not self-reported

100 − staleness − missing_data − disagreement − variance penalties, deterministic and traceable in the execution log — not an LLM guessing a percentage. Mirrors the policy-engine trust story. Weights are tunable heuristics, defensibly so, not claimed to be precisely calibrated.

Scalability shown, not just claimed

Stateless-per-incident workers from Day 1–2 so concurrency is structural. Day 5 stretch goal: trigger two independent incidents live and show both resolve without blocking. Approval-bottleneck-at-scale left as a stated limitation with a ready future-work answer, not built.

Team & schedule, 4 people / 6 days

A: orchestration core · B: mock services + timeout injection · C: frontend wired to real backend · D: integration, demo scripting, DG layer, pitch prep. Golden path (Three-Way Disruption) hard-frozen after Day 3; Day 5 is additive stretch only; Day 6 is rehearsal plus a recorded backup run.

Rejected along the way

  • Single agent + if-engine pretending to be "orchestration" — didn't earn the name.
  • LLM self-reported confidence percentages — not defensible under judge questioning.
  • Agent-to-agent direct messaging between specialists — too much build risk for a hackathon window, replaced with a synthesis arbiter.
  • Berth/crane/yard coordination alone as the "innovation" story — too generic, sounds like any incident-response system.

Weak point that survived scrutiny

Presentation & Clarity was flagged but not worked through this session. The source proposal is 31 dense sections; no pass was made on what actually goes on pitch slides versus what stays as backup material for Q&A. Recommended as the next thing to pressure-test before the pitch deck is finalized.
